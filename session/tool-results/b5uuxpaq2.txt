CROSSCUTTING
 - ONE-DIRECTIONAL FLOW, three absolute rules with documented reasons: usage only from DAR; DAR only from DAB; DAB only from DAS. 'These are the rules the gatekeeper enforces - the absolutes we measure every deviation against.' [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - Each inter-layer movement does exactly ONE job — ingest (source→DAS), integrate (DAS→DAB), specialize (DAB→DAR). Code that ingests-and-integrates, or integrates-and-shapes-for-a-report, collapses layers and is disallowed. [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - The blueprint contains NO exceptions and NO pre-authorized bypass: 'if we do not have an absolute principle that is described - why we have it, what effect it has - we have nothing to break.' Do not build configurable escape hatches, exception flags or 'bypass DAB' modes into the platform or its docs. [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - There is no ML/AI raw-data carve-out. ML consumes DAR like every other requirement; missing inputs are added to DAB first. The named counter-example clause ('Machine Learning applications are allowed to use raw data...') is given as the exception that 'removes the mandate'. [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - Deviations are costed, not blocked: 'we do not argue with the reality, we define the cost that the reality wants to impose on us.' Record which principle breaks, why, how, the effect on maintainability/scalability/adaptability/time-to-market, the reusability lost, and the life-cycle cost — with product owner, requirement owner, DW architect and data engineer all party to the decision. [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - The vision every design decision is judged against: Maintainable, Scalable, Adaptable, Good time to market — 'throughout its whole life cycle', estimated at 15-25 years. Speed is explicitly NOT the top criterion: 'Not the fastest way, but always within acceptable timing.' [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - The blueprint is technology-free and overarching; tooling, modeling technique and process live in subordinate documents that must each be justified against the vision. Any tool (dlt, daana-cli, the USS, the warehouse engine) must be replaceable without invalidating the blueprint. [the-architectural-blueprint]
 - The declaration is the source of truth and the warehouse is an OUTPUT: 'The declared model is the source and the warehouse is an output.' No hand-edits to deployed objects; no round-tripping fixes back from the warehouse into the model. [context-isnt-enough]
 - Definitions ARE the implementation: 'the definition is the implementation. The platform reads those definitions and turns them into the running system. Change a definition, change the system.' There must be no second documentation surface that can drift. [the-work-before-the-answer, context-isnt-enough]
 - The same declarations serve triple duty — what humans read, what LLMs read, and what drives transformation generation: 'These aren't three separate artifacts that can drift - they're the same source.' [why-we-built-daana]
 - 'Naming is uniform because it is generated.' Naming rules live in the compiler, not in style guides each engineer applies. [context-isnt-enough]
 - 'Logic is defined once and compiled wherever it is needed, and nothing deployed can be orphaned, because everything derives from the current model.' Deployment is a complete projection of the current model. [context-isnt-enough]
 - Generation guarantees consistency, NOT correctness: 'A generated platform still inherits the quality of its source systems, and a poorly chosen model compiles just as reliably as a good one.' Modeling judgment and source quality still need human review. [context-isnt-enough]
 - 'A rule the agent has to remember is weaker than a rule the platform enforces.' Whenever a constraint is expressible in the model, express it there rather than in agent instructions, skills or rules files. [context-isnt-enough]
 - 'Context can route an agent to the right answer only if the platform contains a right answer to route to.' Resolve duplicate/near-duplicate definitions in the model rather than routing around them downstream. [context-isnt-enough]
 - Uniformity is an agent-facing FEATURE: 'an agent that learns the shape of one entity knows the shape of all of them.' Per-entity special cases, escape hatches and bespoke overrides erode the property that makes the platform navigable. [context-isnt-enough]
 - The platform must be re-readable as an agent recovery mechanism: semantic model + contracts + column descriptions must be sufficient to fully re-orient an agent with zero conversation history. Nothing load-bearing may live only in chat. [ai-assisted-data-engineering]
 - 'Verify before you generate' is encoded in every agent's workflow, not offered as advice: check INFORMATION_SCHEMA.COLUMN_FIELD_PATHS for column descriptions, read the view definitions, understand what's upstream — before writing SQL. [ai-assisted-data-engineering]
 - Layer boundaries are 'semantic checkpoints' that tell humans and agents what kind of work belongs where; work must be refusable at the wrong layer. [ai-assisted-data-engineering]
 - Layer names encode PURPOSE, not position or quality grade — deliberately not bronze/silver/gold or staging/core/marts, because 'ask three people what belongs in silver and you'll get four answers.' 'That distinction matters when the names are also your principles.' [the-architectural-blueprint, why-we-built-daana, the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer, ai-assisted-data-engineering]
 - The DW's primary job is Information Integration — 'creating a shared view of information that works regardless of where the data originated' — not storage, performance, or dashboards. Both halves are required: model integration (structure) and semantic integration (meaning). Generating a star schema is model integration only. [definitions-for-integration]
 - Acceptance scope is metadata: 'A Definition created for a single division cannot serve as an Enterprise Definition.' The scope a deliverable is advertised at must not exceed the scope of the Definitions behind it. [definitions-for-integration]
 - Build the entire stack THIN first: 'A skeleton at every layer that someone can look at, even if it's wrong. Then iterate against existing trusted reports where possible. Depth comes after the loop closes.' Depth-first on DAS cost ~six weeks and hid a silent identifier collision. [the-work-before-the-answer]
 - Three agent-friendliness requirements for the whole platform: clear semantic definitions (agents cannot intuit from context), explicit rules and boundaries (what's allowed / forbidden / requires approval), and documented processes rather than tribal knowledge. [designing-for-agentic-experience]
 - Two-tier alignment: 'harder' metadata (data contracts, entity definitions) enforces structure mechanically; 'softer' conventions (naming, SQL style) are enforced by a doc-reading agent in CI. [stop-being-naming-convention-police]
 - Model-level validation runs BEFORE code generation, not as SQL tests after deployment; and the entity definitions must live in a queryable model registry that actively drives generation, 'not documentation sites'. [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer]
 - Access model: domain teams get write access to model/contract files only, not arbitrary SQL against production; the central platform team owns patterns, standards and the generation framework. Contract ownership sits with the data-producing product team as the unit of accountability. [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer, the-work-before-the-answer]
 - Tested/target platforms are PostgreSQL and BigQuery; MSSQL and Oracle fail fast with 'not yet supported' at every entry point (previously they fell through to the PostgreSQL executor and produced corruption) but stay authorable in connection profiles via `PlannedDialects`. [daana-cli-0-7-0-release]
 - Declarative is NOT universally better: imperative still wins for one-off analyses, genuinely novel algorithms, performance-critical optimization, early-stage exploration, and very small teams ('three sources, ten entities, one data engineer'). Mature platforms carry both. [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer]
 - Budget weeks-to-months of upfront investment (framework setup, code generation tooling, team training) before the first generated pipeline pays off. Technical debt shifts rather than disappears: the two named failure modes are over-rigid abstractions and overly generic models lacking domain specificity. [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer]
 - Separation of concerns is named as the single hardest architectural decision in daana-cli: 'To make sure each part was doing its own thing and not cluttered with everything.' The user surface is deliberately skewed simple while the engine carries the complexity. [meet-peiman-khorramshahi-cto-at-daana]
 - Do not decompose the data stack with software-engineering modularity (microservices): it 'makes it very hard to solve problems around single source of truth, how to process massive data sets.' Adopt DevOps practice incrementally — 'a delicate maneuver that takes time and will be very costly if rushed.' [meet-lars-fredholm-principal-data-engineer-at-daana]
 - daana-cli is alpha software co-built with the vendor: 'The core patterns work... But translating those into a tool that works for diverse teams requires feedback.' The architectural patterns are the stable part; the tool is not. [why-we-built-daana]

CONTRADICTIONS
 - DAR SHAPE — the single biggest unresolved conflict. consumption-layer-generates-itself says generate a Unified Star Schema: one bridge table, 'you stop sorting tables into facts and dimensions', every entity plays both roles. ai-assisted-data-engineering says DAR is Kimball: a 'Kimball Agent' that 'builds the consumption layer - dimensions, facts, snapshots', with 'conformed dimensions' and 'additive measures'. why-we-built-daana says 'dimensional modeling remains the natural choice for the Data As Requirements (DAR) layer'. These are three different physical designs and no post reconciles them.
 - DAR REUSE — the blueprint forbids what the other DAR posts require. Blueprint: 'No "reuse" between different requirements. Different user groups must not share structures even when requirements align. No intra-dependencies between requirements of different user groups.' But ai-assisted-data-engineering mandates 'conformed dimensions' (a construct whose entire purpose is sharing structure across requirements), and consumption-layer-generates-itself makes every mart a projection/filter of ONE shared bridge, which is shared structure by construction. Deliberate duplication vs one shared model cannot both be right.
 - DAS 'CAPTURE AND PERSIST ONLY' vs CONTRACT-DRIVEN STAGING. The blueprint's DAS Simplicity principle is absolute: 'Capture and persist only. Transformation logic resides in the code that loads DAB.' But rise-of-model-driven, ai-assisted, work-before-the-answer and (by reference) contract-driven-data-transformation all put a substantial transformation step inside DAS: unpacking nested JSON, type casting, renaming camelCase→snake_case, array handling, and generating historized AND latest views. Either the blueprint's DAS is only the landing zone (and staging is unlabelled), or the staging step violates a stated absolute.
 - LAYER NAME EXPANSIONS DIFFER. 'Data According to System / Business / Requirement' [the-architectural-blueprint] vs 'Data As System sees it / Data As Business sees it / Data As Requirements needs it' [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer, why-we-built-daana] vs 'Data As System / Data As Business / Data As Requirements' [ai-assisted-data-engineering] vs 'Data According to Requirements' [consumption-layer-generates-itself]. DAR is also singular 'Requirement' in the blueprint and plural elsewhere. A builder writing documentation has to pick.
 - GENERATED DAB OBJECT NAMES CONFLICT ACROSS THREE POSTS. `SUBSCRIPTION_HIST` / `SUBSCRIPTION_LATEST` (two views per entity) [the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer]; `VIEW_CUSTOMER` / `VIEW_CUSTOMER_HIST` / `VIEW_CUSTOMER_WITH_REL` (three views per entity) [why-we-built-daana]; `view_<entity>_hist` / `<entity>_desc` / `view_<source>_has_<target>` / `_x` / `_idfr`, all lowercase [daana-cli-0-7-0-release]. Likely version drift, but no post says which contract is current, and 0.7.0 explicitly calls the lowercase change breaking for downstream consumers.
 - CASING CONVENTION. Two posts show DMDL ids as double-quoted SCREAMING_SNAKE_CASE with id == name; 0.7.0 says UPPER_SNAKE_CASE was previously enforced, is now optional, and the three casing lint rules were REMOVED — so the platform no longer enforces the convention its own examples use. Simultaneously the DAS contract examples use lowercase snake_case target names, so the platform runs two casing worlds.
 - SNOWFLAKE SUPPORT. why-we-built-daana: 'It works on BigQuery, Snowflake, and other cloud platforms.' daana-cli-0-7-0-release: tested environments are 'latest PostgreSQL and current GCP-default BigQuery'; Snowflake appears only in the TypeTextDDL mapping and dialect-aware TRUNCATE; MSSQL and Oracle fail fast with 'not yet supported' despite having type mappings. The type-mapping table advertises dialects the engine refuses to run.
 - BUILD ORDER. rise-of-model-driven: 'Start with layers where patterns are clearest. Ingestion contracts often provide high return... Core business entities in the DAB layer offer similar benefits once you've established your domain model. Metrics definitions pay off when serving multiple consumption patterns' — a layer-at-a-time order. work-before-the-answer: building DAS 'first, in full, before we had anything end-to-end' was the ~six-week mistake; 'build the entire stack thin first.'
 - ABSTRACT-LATER vs DECLARE-FIRST. rise-of-model-driven: 'Build a few implementations, discover commonalities, then abstract. Premature abstraction before patterns emerge adds overhead without benefit.' Every other post (context-isnt-enough, why-we-built-daana, work-before-the-answer, consumption-layer) treats hand-written implementations as the failure mode and declares from day one — and daana-cli is a pre-built abstraction you adopt before discovering your own patterns.
 - ESCAPE HATCHES. rise-of-model-driven requires them ('generation frameworks that... provid[e] room for custom logic where standard patterns don't fit') while warning 'Too many escape hatches undermine consistency'. The blueprint forbids pre-authorized exceptions entirely. context-isnt-enough says per-entity special cases erode agent legibility. why-we-built-daana admits 'you sacrifice some flexibility in custom transformations' and describes no hatch at all. Four positions, no resolution.
 - IMMUTABILITY vs FULL REFRESH. Blueprint: DAS and DAB are 'Not allowed to update or delete data.' daana-cli 0.7.0 ships `--full-refresh` at workflow/entity/attribute scope, makes drop-and-redeploy of the DAB schema the standard upgrade path, and FULL_LOG pipelines soft-delete rows (`row_st = 'N'`). Rebuild-from-DAS is arguably consistent with 'reload downstream layers with all historical data', but per-attribute full refresh and soft-deletes are not obviously reconcilable with the absolute.
 - ONE HISTORIZATION MECHANISM vs PER-PIPELINE CHOICE. context-isnt-enough: 'Historization is something you declare, with one mechanism everywhere, rather than something each pipeline solves in its own way.' daana-cli-0-7-0: INCREMENTAL vs FULL_LOG is a per-pipeline choice with materially different relationship semantics (forward-only emission with query-time set detection vs. soft-delete on source-row disappearance), and the post does not say which to pick. Additionally, DAS generates historized+latest views AND DAB generates hist/latest views — two historization layers, never reconciled.
 - WHERE MEASURES/METRICS ARE DECLARED. consumption-layer-generates-itself: DAB declares measures ('everything a consumption model needs is already declared'; the walk 'inherits the keys and measures of everything it reaches'). rise-of-model-driven: metrics are a DAR declaration ('MRR equals sum of monthly_value for active subscriptions... grain of monthly'). definitions-for-integration demands Definitions for metrics but its four-level hierarchy has no metric level. Three placements.
 - DIRECT DAB QUERYING. Blueprint (absolute): 'Data usage for analytical purposes is only allowed from the DAR layer', because direct DAB usage 'create[s] implicit dependencies that make the system harder to change'. daana-cli-0-7-0 casually contemplates the forbidden practice: 'If you query DAB directly from BI, plan a window accordingly.'
 - NAMING ENFORCEMENT. context-isnt-enough: 'Naming is uniform because it is generated' — there is nothing left to police. stop-being-naming-convention-police: build a CI agent that fetches a Confluence/Notion convention page and enforces it on every PR. That page is a second documentation surface, which context-isnt-enough and work-before-the-answer explicitly forbid ('documentation can't drift because it's the same artifact as the code'). Both cannot be the design.
 - MODEL REGISTRY LOCATION. rise-of-model-driven: 'Platforms need model registries storing entity definitions as queryable metadata - not documentation sites, but active systems driving code generation.' daana-cli-0-7-0: 'the source of truth lives in your version-controlled YAML' AND there is a warehouse metadata schema populated at deploy time that the generator reads. Which is authoritative, and which is queryable, is never settled.
 - M:N RELATIONSHIPS. daana-cli 0.7.0 adds first-class M:N support (set-oriented `view_<source>_has_<target>`, fingerprint-based set-change detection, relationship-only mapping tables). consumption-layer-generates-itself says the USS generator refuses to walk m:n edges and reports them as skipped, and that you must 'model around' m:n in DAB. So the DAB tool now supports a construct the DAR generator cannot consume.
 - IDENTIFIERS ALWAYS vs IDFR OPT-IN. context-isnt-enough: 'Every entity gets identifiers from the start.' integration-by-design: 'Treat multi-identifier resolution as opt-in: turn it on only for entities that genuinely have multiple, source-spanning identifiers' — for a single-key entity 'the pipeline should be skipped entirely'. Reconcilable (identity ≠ the multi-identifier pipeline) but easy to misapply, and neither post says how single-key entities get their surrogate key.
 - CONTRACT GRANULARITY. work-before-the-answer: 'a data contract for each source, owned by the data producing product team'. ai-assisted-data-engineering: 'Each contract specifies a single source-to-staging transformation: which raw table to read...' One-per-source vs one-per-table changes the whole DAS directory layout and ownership model.
 - GENERATED SQL: UNIFORM vs RENAMEABLE. integration-by-design says the surrogate key column name is a convention you should 'rename to whatever fits your code standards' and that `_idfr` columns beyond IDFR and KEY are optional/negotiable. context-isnt-enough says naming and structure are uniform precisely because they are generated and that per-entity variation destroys agent legibility. A configurable generator and a uniform platform pull against each other.
 - DEBUGGABILITY vs GENERATION. work-before-the-answer resolves the black-box objection with 'The business logic is still fully visible, the transformations are still there, you just don't need to write the boilerplate code' and rise-of-model-driven requires generated SQL to stay readable and traceable. But the corpus also insists generated SQL is disposable artifact you never read or edit, and daana-cli 0.7.0's generated pipeline SQL is regenerated per execute and cached for speed. Whether generated SQL is a reviewed surface is unresolved.
 - TWO CODE-GENERATION CHAINS, NEVER RECONCILED. ai-assisted-data-engineering describes both 'A Python generator reads these contracts and produces SQLX files that Dataform executes against BigQuery' and 'YAML contracts fed a compiler producing JSON, which fed a calculation engine generating SQL for BigQuery' — and never says whether these are the same pipeline, or one for DAS staging and one for DAB mappings.
 - AGENT AUTONOMY. designing-for-agentic-experience prescribes progressive trust: tight oversight → documented process + spot checks → independent specialized agents. ai-assisted-data-engineering describes agents already running mandatory protocols and refusing to skip tests. meet-peiman offers 'fully autonomous agents that can build your whole stack with super high quality just by asking the business people questions' as a hot take. The corpus spans manual walkthroughs to full autonomy with no stated criterion for graduation.
 - DEFINITIONS: MACHINE-ACCESSIBLE vs FREE TEXT. definitions-for-integration requires Definitions at four levels (Concept, Characteristic, Domain, Value) plus relationships and metrics, all 'accessible to both humans and AI systems'. DMDL provides only free-text `definition` and `description` strings on entities, attributes and relationships — no Domain object, no per-Value definitions, no allowed-values key (enum vocabularies are carried as prose inside description strings), no acceptance-scope field. The required model and the shown model do not match.
 - 'NO REUSE BETWEEN REQUIREMENTS' vs 'LOGIC DEFINED ONCE'. Blueprint DAR forbids shared structures across user groups; context-isnt-enough says 'Logic is defined once and compiled wherever it is needed.' Reconcilable if 'logic is shared, structures are duplicated' — but no post states that reconciliation, and a builder generating one bridge for everyone will violate the blueprint.
 - BLUEPRINT SAYS NO TECHNOLOGY, THE REST OF THE CORPUS IS TECHNOLOGY. The blueprint insists the overarching document 'contains nothing about technology, processes, modeling methods, or design patterns' and that every tool must be replaceable without invalidating it. Yet the corpus's operative constraints (lowercase framework columns, `ver_tmstp` incremental predicates, IDFR slot counts, the Puppini bridge) are deeply tool-specific. A builder cannot both honour the blueprint's technology-neutrality and follow the concrete guidance.
