########## TENSIONS ##########

* blueprint.md F1 — 'Analytical usage reads only DAR.'
  strain: The already-decided acceptance mechanism computes every question twice, once from das__staged and once from dar__uss. Computing a business answer from das__staged is, read plainly, the thing F1 forbids. Worse than the flow breach is the definitional one: the control query is a second expression of 'orders placed per order month and ship country', written in DAS source vocabulary, living outside dab/model.yaml — exactly what B8 exists to prevent ('Definitions that exist in two places drift'). The control query's purpose is to surface drift, but nothing in the repo says which side is authoritative when they disagree, and §6 forbids fixing a failing check by weakening it. There is also a shelf life: the das__staged expression stays writable only while a question needs no integration. From the first question that spans two sources, writing it against das__staged means reimplementing DAB inside the check.
  -> Register a deviation. This is F1 exactly, done knowingly, for a good reason — not a case the rule was never aimed at. The tempting alternative is to clarify that a CI check is not 'analytical usage'; it is defensible (F1 says analytical usage, and a regression check is engineering usage) and it is what most teams would do, but it works by narrowing a word in an absolute rule to make the thing you want fall outside it, and the next person uses the same move for a notebook that 'is just a check'. The register's own words settle it: an unregistered exception is indistinguishable from a mistake. Recommended entry — Cost: every question carries a second definition in source vocabulary that must be maintained in lockstep, and a disagreement has no declared arbiter. Exit condition: the first question whose das__staged expression cannot be written without reimplementing DAB integration; at that point the control query is deleted and replaced by machinery tests of the generator.

* conventions.md §1.2 — DAB identifiers are UPPER_SNAKE_CASE, with id and name holding the identical string.
  strain: Spike 2 F3 observed that DuckDB is case-preserving, so model.yaml casing lands verbatim in object names: 'Entity id CUSTOMER produced view_CUSTOMER, CUSTOMER_focal, ORDER_CUSTOMER_x — not the lowercase view_customer the PostgreSQL run produced.' Slice 1 therefore gets dab.view_ORDER, dab.ORDER_desc, ORDER_key, and — since the spike's working model used lowercase attribute ids and produced lowercase columns — UPPER attribute ids will produce columns ORDER_ID, SHIP_COUNTRY, PLACED_ON. Every DAR statement must then double-quote them (§3), RF06 must stay excluded (it already is, and spike 4 confirms six hits in a six-column view), and spike 2's gotcha warns that 'code that string-matches names out of information_schema WILL break' — which the USS generator must do, and which is further complicated by spike 2 F13: the views are wrappers over table macros that do not appear in information_schema at all. Separately, the rule's stated reason — 'It is the modelling language's convention' — is no longer true of the tool: the blog records that daana-cli 0.7.0 accepts any casing and removed the entity-name-casing, attribute-type-casing and relationship-name-casing lint rules.
  -> Clarify the rule. Keep UPPER_SNAKE_CASE — its real value is the second reason given, that the visual break makes a leak obvious on sight, and that reason survives the tool's change. Add three sentences: the rule governs identifiers in dab/model.yaml and dab/uss.yaml only; the generator lower-cases on the way into DAR (§1.3 already says this for attribute columns, not for object or key names — say it for those too); and any read of the catalog is case-normalised and must also query duckdb_functions(), because the macros the views depend on are invisible to information_schema. The honest alternative is to change the rule to lowercase ids: it would delete an entire class of quoting bugs, remove the need to exclude RF06, and cost nothing with the tool as it now stands. Reject it, but on the record — it also deletes the property that makes a business term leaking into another layer visible at a glance, and makes the DAB identifier and the DAR object name textually identical.

* blueprint.md S1 — 'Capture and persist only. No business logic, no interpretation, no enrichment.'
  strain: das__staged unpacks the payload and casts: OrderDate to DATE, Freight to DECIMAL. A cast is a decision, and spike 1 F16 shows it is a consequential one — dlt infers freight as DOUBLE, the explicit cast makes it DECIMAL(18,4), and the finding calls that 'a concrete argument for the two-layer design'. It is also an interpretation of what the source meant by a number. The blueprint contains both S1 (no interpretation) and S3 (enforce structure at the boundary out), and the blog corpus records the same collision as unresolved: the synthesis lists it under contradictions — 'Either the blueprint's DAS is only the landing zone (and staging is unlabelled), or the staging step violates a stated absolute.' The sharpest instance in slice 1 is null handling: Northwind's $metadata declares OrderDate as a nullable Edm.Date, so a cast that silently yields NULL asserts 'this order has no date', and a cast that raises makes a schema commitment at the boundary out.
  -> Clarify the rule — the blueprint is the fixed point and cannot be edited to fit. Add to conventions §1.1 the line the blueprint deliberately leaves out: structural conformance is not interpretation. A cast to the type the source's own metadata declares is transcription and is required; a cast that chooses between plausible types, a COALESCE, a default value, a rename, or a row filter is business logic and belongs in DAB. Northwind publishes $metadata (Edm.Int32, Edm.Date, Edm.String, Edm.Decimal), so slice 1's contract can be justified as transcription of a published declaration rather than a judgement — which is a strong position and worth writing down while it is true. If the team is unwilling to hold that line, the honest move is a deviation, not silence.

* blueprint.md R4 — 'Volatile and generated. Nothing here is hand-written or hand-edited', and conventions.md §1.3 'Nothing in this layer is hand-edited.'
  strain: The repo already carries an empty dar/sql/ directory and .gitignore excludes the warehouse but not generated SQL, so slice 1 will commit generator output. Committing is not editing, but it creates a second copy of the truth with nothing checking it: nothing in the repo asserts that dar/sql at HEAD equals what the generator emits from dab/model.yaml and dab/uss.yaml at HEAD. Spike 4 compounds it by recommending 'run sqlfluff fix as a generation post-step rather than teaching the generator about newlines' — if fix runs outside the generator, the committed artefact is not generator output and regeneration produces a spurious diff every time. Meanwhile §3's whole justification is that generated-SQL diffs are read constantly, which only pays off if the committed file is trustworthy.
  -> Clarify the rule, and add the missing mechanism. Write into conventions §1.3 that generated artefacts are committed and that the gate regenerates and fails on any difference (a regenerate step followed by git diff --exit-code). That keeps R4 literally true — the file is never the source — preserves the diff-readability §3 is built around, and forces sqlfluff fix to live inside the generator rather than in someone's hands. The alternative, gitignoring dar/sql like the .duckdb file, is cleaner against R4 but throws away reviewable diffs of the one layer nobody writes by hand, and makes a generator regression invisible until someone runs it. Recommend committing plus the regeneration check; name its cost, which is a slower gate and a build step that must be deterministic down to whitespace.

* blueprint.md S4 — 'Every data point records where it came from and when.'
  strain: The landed row is payload plus dlt's two columns. Spike 1 F9 is explicit: 'dlt adds EXACTLY TWO metadata columns to a root table: _dlt_load_id and _dlt_id. Nothing else. They are NOT sufficient for which load, from where, when.' The decided design elects _dlt_load_id as the single provenance fact, which answers when (load time, not source event time) and which load, but not from where — no source system, no request URL, no entity — except by inference from the table name and the partition path, neither of which is a column. Spike 1 F18 shows the conforming option was available and declined: hand-added das_source_system, das_request_url, das_http_status, das_page_number all 'pass through untouched'. Second, S4 says data point, not row. One blob per record makes a field's provenance the record's provenance — which is true only while one contract reads one endpoint into one row, and stops being true the moment a landed row composes two responses.
  -> Clarify the rule, with a condition attached. Write into conventions §1.1 that a landed record is by construction one whole source record from one response, so record-level provenance is per-data-point provenance; that composing a landed row from more than one response is forbidden, because it silently voids that equivalence; and that 'from where' is carried by the contract stem and the partition path, naming why no source-identity column exists (one source, one endpoint per contract). If the team is not willing to forbid composition outright, this is not a clarification but a deviation and must be registered as one. Do not leave it as it is: as written, the design satisfies S4 by an argument nobody has written down, which is the same as not satisfying it.

* conventions.md §5 — 'No data vocabulary anywhere under src/ or in test code.'
  strain: The slice's acceptance is that two business queries agree. Those queries are pure business vocabulary — ship_country, order_date, _measure__order__placed_orders_count. As literals in tests/ they break the rule outright, and moving them to src/ breaks the same rule. The rule already names its own escape ('Test fixtures use neutral models held as data files'), and README.md already promises docs/questions/ holding 'the business questions, and the two queries that must agree on each answer' — but conventions.md says nothing about what a question file is, where it lives, or that it is the vehicle that keeps the test generic. Slice 1 will invent that format silently, in the one place the whole slice is accepted.
  -> Clarify the rule. State that a question is a data file under docs/questions/ carrying the question text, the two SQL bodies and the expected shape; that the runner in tests/ discovers question files and asserts agreement without naming anything; and that no test may contain an entity, attribute, measure or source name. Pin the file format in §4 while you are there. Two consequences worth writing down: the question SQL must be .sql files so the existing sqlfluff pre-commit hook (types: [sql]) lints them like everything else — §3's 'SQL that lives in a Python string is written to a file' already demands this — and the runner must be parameterised, because a hand-written per-question test function reintroduces the vocabulary through its own name.

* conventions.md §6 — data checks assert what the built warehouse contains, no pytest.skip and no imperative xfail; and §7 — every commit passes the gate and the whole suite on its own.
  strain: The gate is `uv run pre-commit run --all-files`, whose pytest hook runs the whole suite with pass_filenames: false. The warehouse is gitignored (*.duckdb) so it is never in a fresh checkout, and CI does nothing but sync and run the gate. From the first commit of slice 1 the data check therefore runs with no warehouse: it cannot skip (§6), it cannot pass, and 'every commit is green' collapses. Building the warehouse inside every gate run is not free either — spike 2 F11 found that any other open connection, 'read-write OR READ-ONLY', makes daana-cli execute fail with the misleading 'daana framework is not installed on the remote database', and spike 4 notes sqlfluff is already the slowest hook.
  -> Clarify the rule and make the configuration honest — which §6 explicitly permits as the alternative to weakening a test. Split the suite: the local pre-commit pytest hook runs machinery tests only; CI builds the warehouse from das/fixtures before running the gate, then runs the data checks as an explicitly-invoked suite. That requires §7's 'the whole suite' to say which suite, and requires the build step to exist in ci.yml before slice 1's first data check lands. Reject the tempting shortcut of a fixture that fabricates a warehouse for the check: §6's second row forbids exactly that ('Data checks are not written against a stub'), and a check that passes against a stub tells you nothing about the build.

* conventions.md §8 — a departure from a principle needs an entry in deviations.md and an ADR; §7 — an ADR lands before the code it decides; deviations.md — 'Every deviation has one; the register is the index, the ADR is the argument.'
  strain: D-0001 cites adr/0002-dar-is-one-generated-unified-star-schema.md. That file does not exist. docs/adr/ contains only 0000-use-madr.md, and docs/adr/README.md's index lists only 0000. So the one open deviation in the register — the one slice 1's entire DAR design rests on — currently has no argument behind it and is not in the index. By the register's own review standard ('a defect in the same way a failing test is'), this is already broken, and slice 1 is the commit that would build on it.
  -> Not one of the three recourses — it is a defect to fix, and it blocks. ADR 0002 must land before any dar-scoped code in this slice, together with whatever 0001 is meant to be, and docs/adr/README.md's index must list them. Recommend treating the missing ADR as a gate on the dar commit specifically, not on the slice as a whole, so das and dab work is not held hostage to it.

* conventions.md §1.2 — 'No source name appears anywhere in DAB — not in an id, not in a definition, not in a description.'
  strain: dab/mappings/orders-mapping.yaml must contain `table: das__staged.orders`, `primary_keys: [order_id]` and `transformation_expression: ship_country` — source words, in files under dab/. Spike 2 F2 shows exactly that shape working. Read literally, §1.2 is violated by every mapping file the system will ever contain, and the first reviewer applying it as a hard rule blocks the first mapping in the slice. The same problem reaches dab/uss.yaml, which sits in dab/ but declares DAR things and must follow §1.3 and §2 rather than §1.2.
  -> Clarify the rule. The rule is aimed at the vocabulary DAB publishes; the mapping is the one artefact whose entire job is to bind two vocabularies and it cannot do that without naming both. Write the line explicitly: source names may appear in dab/mappings/** in `table`, `primary_keys` and `transformation_expression` and nowhere else; dab/model.yaml carries no source name in an id, name, definition or description; dab/uss.yaml is governed by §1.3 and §2, not §1.2, because it is a DAR declaration that happens to live beside the model. Without those three sentences the directory has two naming regimes and nothing says which key falls under which.

* conventions.md §1.3 'Measure columns are _measure__<entity>__<measure>' against §2 'Pattern: <descriptor>_<entities>_<unit>', entity part plural.
  strain: Slice 1's single measure resolves to _measure__order__placed_orders_count — the entity appears twice, singular from §1.3 and plural inside the measure name from §2. The blog's own worked example is _measure__order_line__revenue: a bare measure word in the third segment, not the three-part name. So the convention document and the source it derives from disagree about what the third segment holds, and slice 1 must encode one of them in the generator, permanently, on its first run.
  -> Clarify the rule — say which, in §1.3, with an example. Recommend keeping the full §2 name in the third segment (_measure__order__placed_orders_count): §2 is a hard rule whose stated payoff is searchability 'by any of the three' parts, and that only holds if the whole name survives into the physical column. Name the cost honestly: the columns get long and repetitive, and §1.3's claim that two underscores make the three segments unambiguous gets harder to read once the third segment contains single underscores of its own.

* blueprint.md B3 — 'Time-variant. Historized: every version retained, none overwritten', with its reason 'An answer served last quarter must still be reproducible this quarter, or the system cannot be audited.'
  strain: Spike 2 F9 observed that ingestion_strategy: FULL against a source view holding more than one row per primary key inserts 'exactly ONE duplicate _desc row per execute — same key, same type_key, same seq_nbr, same eff_tmstp, same value', growing 22 → 23 → 24 → 25 across three runs, and that 'NONE of the presentation views show it: view_CUSTOMER stayed at 3 rows and view_CUSTOMER_hist at 4 rows the whole time'. The decided DAS design — append-only parquet, one row per key per load — makes das__staged.orders multi-row-per-key by construction, which is precisely the triggering input. Nothing in conventions.md constrains the strategy, so slice 1 picks one silently. The compounding problem is that the slice's headline safety net is blind to it: both the das__staged control query and the dar__uss query read through views that mask the duplication, so the two queries agree while the history quietly corrupts.
  -> Clarify the rule — add a hard rule to conventions §1.2 or a new mapping section: mappings use FULL_LOG; FULL is permitted only against a source view proven to hold exactly one row per key, and the proof is a data check, not a claim. Pair it with a data check that counts dab.<ENTITY>_desc rows across two consecutive executes and fails on growth, because that base table is the only place the defect is visible. Also record in the DAB ADR that this is an unadvertised-platform risk: spike 2 notes daana-cli's own help text lists PostgreSQL, BigQuery, Snowflake, Oracle and SQL Server and does not mention DuckDB, so 'do not expect DuckDB parity to be maintained by the vendor'.

* The decided constraint 'One DuckDB file, platform behind one seam', together with conventions.md §7 'every commit passes the gate'.
  strain: Spike 2 F11 found that any other open connection to the file — including a read-only one — makes daana-cli execute fail, and that the error it reports is a lie: 'daana framework is not installed on the remote database. Run daana-cli install first', with no lock, conflict or duckdb string anywhere even at debug level. Slice 1 puts three processes on one file: dlt writing, daana-cli writing, marimo reading. Spike 3's gotcha is the mirror image — 'a marimo app holding a read-write handle will block dlt or daana-cli'. Nothing in the repo states a writer discipline, so the first CI failure will be misdiagnosed as a broken install.
  -> Clarify the rule — this is a design constraint the documents simply do not carry. Write into conventions (or the CLI ADR, where the seam is decided) that the warehouse has exactly one writer, that adss build holds it exclusively and stops before opening it if anything else has it, that every destination opens with read_only=True, and that 'daana framework is not installed on the remote database' means the file is busy. Three sentences now save a debugging session per person who meets it, and spike 2 F12 confirms the failure is transient rather than corrupting, so the fix really is discipline rather than repair.

* conventions.md §1.1 — 'Columns this layer adds itself carry no source's word and are named for what they record: extracted_at, extracted_on, payload', with blueprint.md's opening 'Every tool this system currently uses must be replaceable without invalidating a single line below.'
  strain: The decided provenance design makes _dlt_load_id the single provenance fact. It is not on §1.1's list, it carries a tool's word rather than a source's, and it is not named for what it records — it is named for who wrote it. If it surfaces in das__staged it welds dlt's name into the layer's published interface, which is exactly what the blueprint's replaceability sentence forbids, and §1.1's own reason ('Provenance is ours, not the source's, and must be tellable apart from it at a glance') argues against it.
  -> Clarify the rule. State that _dlt_load_id is the raw layer's own bookkeeping and does not cross into das__staged: the staged view exposes extracted_at and extracted_on only, both derived from it via to_timestamp(cast(_dlt_load_id AS DOUBLE)). The derivation is injective so nothing is lost — a specific load package is still reachable from extracted_at. Cost: one more expression in every staged view, and das__raw remains tool-shaped, which is correct because das__raw is where the tool lives. Recommend it; without the sentence, slice 1 will pass _dlt_load_id straight through and the seam is breached on day one, in the layer everything else is rebuilt from.

* blueprint.md B8 ('Meaning is defined exactly once') and R4 (the generator's output is not a place for judgement), applied to the question's own dimensions.
  strain: Northwind's $metadata declares both of slice 1's dimensions as nullable: <Property Name="OrderDate" Type="Edm.Date" /> and <Property Name="ShipCountry" Type="Edm.String" /> — neither carries Nullable="false", unlike OrderId. So the generator must decide what a bridge event with no date is, and what a peripheral row with no country groups under. Drop the row, keep it with a null _event_date, or invent an 'Unknown' calendar member — each is a business decision with no definition behind it in model.yaml. Worse, the two acceptance queries will disagree by construction if they treat nulls differently: a GROUP BY on das__staged keeps the null group, a join to a calendar drops it. The check catches the disagreement and the fix has no home.
  -> Clarify the rule — conventions needs a null rule for the USS, and slice 1 is where it gets set. Recommend: the bridge keeps the event with a null _event_date and a null key, and the generator never invents an 'Unknown' member, because an invented member is meaning with no definition behind it and B8 says meaning has exactly one home. The question file then states its own filter explicitly, and both queries inherit it from the same file rather than each making a choice. Cost: the page must show the null group or exclude it visibly, and the question file grows a field — which is the right place for it, since the choice is a property of the question and not of the model.

* conventions.md §3 — 'No SELECT * in anything a consumer reads, except a union of stages whose column list the generator fixes.'
  strain: Spike 2 F13 observed that on DuckDB daana's presentation views are literally CREATE VIEW dab.view_CUSTOMER AS SELECT * FROM dab.f_CUSTOMER(). dar__uss reads dab.view_ORDER, so DAR — a consumer of DAB — reads through a star this repository did not write and cannot lint. The rule's stated reason lands squarely: add an attribute to model.yaml, redeploy, and the shape underneath every DAR statement changes with nothing flagging it.
  -> Clarify the rule. Say that §3 governs SQL this repository emits, that third-party generated objects are outside its reach, and that the mitigation is a rule the generator must obey: the USS generator never selects * from a DAB view — it names every column from dab/model.yaml, so a DAB schema change shows up as a regeneration diff rather than as a silent shape change. Record the residual cost plainly: the repository cannot enforce §3 on dab.*, and a DAB change is caught only when DAR is regenerated, which is one more reason the regeneration check in the gate is not optional.

########## D-0001 REVIEW ##########
 "D-0001's exit condition is: 'A second consumer needs isolation: a different shape of the same structure, or a group that must not see part of it. The generator then gains per-group projections over the same bridge and this entry is resolved.' It has NOT been met, and the entry correctly remains open. There is not yet a first consumer, let alone a second: destinations/ is an empty untracked directory, dar/sql is empty, and no marimo page, no dar__uss and no generator exist. Slice 1 creates the first consumer. One consumer cannot need isolation from itself.\n\nThree things the review turns up that the exit condition alone does not.\n\nFirst, the entry is defective as it stands, independently of its exit condition. It cites adr/0002-dar-is-one-generated-unified-star-schema.md; that file does not exist, and docs/adr/README.md's index lists only 0000. The register's own field spec says 'ADR — the decision record that argued it. Every deviation has one; the register is the index, the ADR is the argument.' Right now the index points at nothing. By the register's own review standard — 'a defect in the same way a failing test is' — this is already failing, and conventions.md §7 ('An ADR lands before the code it decides') makes it blocking for slice 1's dar-scoped work specifically. Write ADR 0002 before any DAR code, and add it to the index.\n\nSecond, the entry's justification is not exercised by the slice that first depends on it. The 'Why' argues the shared structure is safe because 'keys inherit along many-to-one relationships, measures stay on the stage that owns them — so the fan trap R2 exists to prevent cannot occur in it'. Slice 1 has one entity and no relationships, so nothing inherits and there is no fan-out to be safe from: the bridge is one stage over one event, and the walk the argument rests on has zero edges. The deviation is therefore being carried on a property that is asserted rather than demonstrated until slice 2 introduces a second entity and an n:1 edge. ADR 0002 should say this in as many words, and slice 1 should add a machinery test that a zero-edge walk is a valid walk, so slice 2 does not discover it as a special case.\n\nThird, the entry's stated cost — 'the first thing to break is a slice needing a measure defined one way for one reader and another way for another' — has no pressure on it yet: one reader, one measure. But the tripwire is worth writing down now: this entry should be re-read the moment a second destination appears, not only when isolation is actually demanded, because by then the shared bridge will already have shaped both readers. Recommend adding that to the register's Review section as a standing instruction, since 'read at the start of every slice' currently only tests the exit condition and would have missed the missing ADR entirely."

########## GAPS ##########
 - What a question is, and where it lives. README.md already promises docs/questions/ holding 'the business questions, and the two queries that must agree on each answer', and conventions.md §5 requires test fixtures to be 'neutral models held as data files' — but nothing says what a question file contains, how it is named, or that it is the artefact that keeps the acceptance test free of business vocabulary. Slice 1 invents this format in the one place the whole slice is accepted.
 - Whether generated artefacts are committed, and what proves they still match the model. No rule anywhere, and no regeneration check. Without it, dar/sql becomes a second copy of the truth that nothing compares against dab/model.yaml, and spike 4's recommended 'sqlfluff fix as a generation post-step' guarantees a spurious diff on every regeneration.
 - No rule for ingestion_strategy. FULL versus FULL_LOG is, on the evidence of spike 2 F9, a silent-unbounded-corruption choice against exactly the kind of append-shaped staged view this architecture produces, and the presentation views hide it completely. The document that governs every other naming and style choice says nothing about the one setting with an observed data-destroying failure mode.
 - No rule for deduplication between das__raw and das__staged. Spike 1's open question is unanswered: 'Two runs gave 240 rows / 120 distinct orders. Does DAB want the full append history, or a latest-version view? This changes whether das__staged is a plain cast view or a QUALIFY/ROW_NUMBER view.' The blog is unambiguous that a contract generates both — 'create historized and latest views' (the-rise-of-model-driven-data-engineer) — and conventions names neither. The DAB strategy choice depends on the answer, so slice 1 cannot avoid deciding it.
 - No rule requiring union_by_name => true on the parquet glob. Spike 1 F14 is a correctness finding, not a style one: 'a plain read_parquet glob over an append-only lake SILENTLY DROPS columns added in later loads — no error, just missing data.' This belongs in §3 beside RF02, the other rule the document keeps because it is about correctness rather than taste.
 - No rule about path resolution in DuckDB views. Spike 1's gotcha: 'Relative paths inside a DuckDB view's read_parquet() resolve against the DuckDB process's cwd at QUERY time, not at CREATE VIEW time. A view created from the pipeline directory will break when marimo opens the same warehouse.duckdb from elsewhere.' Slice 1 has precisely that shape — built from the repo root, read from destinations/ — and spike 3 repeats it independently: anchor on Path(__file__).parent, never the cwd.
 - No single-writer rule and no read-only rule for destinations, despite two spikes finding the same failure from opposite ends, and despite the error message being actively misleading.
 - No ruff configuration for marimo notebooks. pyproject's per-file-ignores covers tests/* only. Spike 4's open question flags that marimo cells are decorated functions that will trip ANN201 — and ANN is a hard rule the document refuses to drop because it closes ty's hole — and that marimo rewrites the file on save, fighting ruff format. §5 says the tool config is the rule, so the gap is literally in the config and will surface on slice 1's first commit of a page.
 - No rule about tool vocabulary in machinery. §5 forbids data vocabulary under src/ and says nothing about tool names, while the blueprint opens by demanding that every tool be replaceable 'without invalidating a single line below'. The seam is a decided constraint with no written rule behind it, which is how _dlt_load_id ends up in a published view.
 - Nothing implements R5 — 'What was delivered, to whom, and when is recorded.' The slice's deliverable is a rendered page and the repo has no delivery record of any kind; .gitignore even excludes .screenshots/ with the note that screenshots are attached to a pull request rather than committed. Either slice 1 gives R5 a mechanism or the ADR states plainly that R5 is unimplemented and why — but it should not pass unmentioned in the slice that first delivers something.
 - No null-handling rule, in either DAS casts or the USS. Both of slice 1's dimensions are nullable in the source metadata, so the question is live in the first slice, and the two-query check will expose any inconsistency without saying where the fix belongs.
 - No rule saying which naming regime governs dab/uss.yaml. It lives under dab/, where §1.2 mandates UPPER_SNAKE_CASE and bans source names, but it declares measures and events governed by §2 and §1.3. Two regimes, one directory, nothing arbitrating.
 - No layout rule for das/fixtures, and no definition of what 'recorded once and replayed everywhere' means physically — what is recorded, at what granularity, how a replay is distinguished from a live run, and what makes CI provably offline. The directory exists and is empty; CI has no build step; slice 1 invents all of it and every later slice inherits it.
 - No rule for what happens when the two queries disagree. §6 says a failing check is fixed by fixing the code and never by weakening the test, but with two hand-written expressions of one question, 'the code' is ambiguous by construction. The document needs to name the arbiter — DAB's definition — and say that a disagreement is resolved by changing whichever query departs from it, never by adjusting both until they match.
